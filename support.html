Support

Found a bug, have an idea for a hard moment we should cover, or want to tell us how it went? We'd love to hear from you.

Get in touch

Email bugjugapp@gmail.com. It helps to tell us your device, the chapter or place in the story, and what you expected to happen. If you use GitHub, you can also open an issue.

Common questions

What ages is it for?

Kids about 5 to 9. Every line is spoken and every choice has a picture, so children who aren't reading yet can play on their own. Older kids who find feelings tricky often enjoy it too.

Is it only for kids with ADHD or autism?

No. It was designed with ADHD and autistic kids in mind (body signals taught directly, no masking, literal-language jokes, quiet options everywhere), and it works for any kid who feels things big.

How do I get to the parent area?

On the title screen, tap the gear (⚙️) in the bottom corner, then answer a quick grown-ups question. There you'll find the family words guide, your child's Calm Kit, how each kind of hard moment is going, a real-life Forecast and the settings.

Can my child use the Calm Kit during a real meltdown?

Yes, that's what it's for. The big Calm Kit button on the title screen opens it in one tap, and one more tap starts a tool. On its own, the kit is quiet: no story, no questions and no praise. In the parent area you can hide tools that don't suit your child.

What happens if my child picks the "wrong" choice?

There's no wrong-answer buzzer. Most choices are all good ones. The wobbly "itchy" option (push, grab, yell) plays out the way it really would, then Drizzle rewinds for a do-over so your child can try another way. Feeling words that don't fit simply go away.

How do I limit play time?

In the parent area's Settings, set Play time. Mormor gives a warning near the end, and the story stops at the next natural break, never in the middle of a hard moment.

How do we use it outside the game?

Try the game's words at home: "How's your weather?", "body first", and "big feeling or big problem?" The family words guide in the parent area explains each one, and it's handy to share with teachers, OTs or therapists so everyone uses the same language.

Does it need the internet?

No. Frelard Feelings works fully offline, even in airplane mode.

Can we start over?

Yes. In the parent area's Settings, Start her progress over clears chapters, stamps, feeling words, help levels and check-ins. You can also unlock every chapter there.

Terms

Frelard Feelings is a game for practising feelings skills. It is not therapy, medical advice or a diagnosis, and it isn't a substitute for a doctor, therapist or teacher. If you're worried about your child, please talk to a professional.

"Frelard" is a local nickname for the area between Fremont and Ballard in Seattle. The game is set in real public places there. The characters, homes and shops are made up, and any resemblance to real people is a coincidence.
